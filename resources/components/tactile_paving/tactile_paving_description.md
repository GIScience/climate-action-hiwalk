The map categorises pedestrian infrastructure based on its tactile paving.
Because tactile paving is relevant especially at pedestrian crossings, railway or public transport platforms, and stairs, only these features are included in the map.
Tactile paving increases the safety for people with visual impairments.
In contrast, missing or damaged tactile paving may make walking more unsafe and difficult for them.

Explanation of the different tactile paving categories:
* Yes: Tactile paving is present.
* Other signs: No tactile paving is present, but other detectable features such as water drains or decorative elements that can be used for orientation.
* Partial/ Incorrect: Tactile paving is used but not in a sensible way, e.g. tactile paving on only one side of the crossing or tactile paving that leads nowhere.
* No: No tactile paving is present.
* Unknown: Infrastructure for which there is no tactile paving information.

Data come from OpenStreetMap (OSM) and the categorisation is based on OSM tactile paving tags.
